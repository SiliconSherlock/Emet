About this site

Each claim has an ID, the verses it cites, and the TaNaKh passages listed against it. It holds verse citations, not commentary.

  • The verses a claim cites are given in the numbering of the King James Version, and quoted from it.
  • TaNaKh passages use the Hebrew verse numbering of the texts quoted, which sometimes differ from the King James Version; where they do, the passage says so.
  • Some claims carry a source check in fixed wording: the claim cites no verse of the TaNaKh; the words it quotes are not in the TaNaKh; it follows the ancient Greek translation rather than the Hebrew; its words differ from the Hebrew; or it names the wrong book. In each case the Hebrew text is on the page to compare.
  • 0 of 645 passages name a source, and 0 of 645 have been verified against that source. A verified passage is marked ✓.

The book

Everything on this site is also available as a book to read or print: one claim per page, the Hebrew and English side by side verse by verse, with contents and an index. Download the book (PDF).

Finding a claim

  • Type a claim's number after the site's address, for example /42.
  • On the front page, search by number, words or verse, and filter by book: claims that cite the book, or that are answered from it. On a wide screen the claim you pick opens in a reader beside the list.
  • Or look up a passage in the passage index.

Texts quoted

  • English TaNaKh: The Holy Scriptures According to the Masoretic Text: A New Translation (Jewish Publication Society, 1917). Public domain. Text from Sefaria.
  • Hebrew TaNaKh: Tanach with Nikkud. Public domain. Text from Sefaria.
  • Verses cited by the claims: King James Version. Public domain. Text from bible-api.com.

The JPS 1917 translation is used because it is in the public domain and follows the Hebrew verse numbering.

The site is self-contained: every text it quotes is stored in it, and it links to no other website, so nothing on it can go out of date because another site changed.

Privacy and safety

This site is static: plain HTML pages, one stylesheet, and one short script that runs the search and filters on the front page. It sets no cookies, collects nothing, sends nothing, and loads nothing from any other website. Each page carries a Content Security Policy that tells the browser to enforce this.
